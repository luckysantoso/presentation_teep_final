01/Background

The Status Quo

Why Centralized RMFS is Fragile

Single point of failure

Modern RMFS deployments (Amazon Robotics, Geek+) are orchestrated by a central planner — a MAPF solver (CBS, BCP, A*) that computes paths for the entire fleet simultaneously.

  1. One computer dictates the path of every robot in the fleet.
  2. Continuous communication required — TCP backbone, no graceful degradation.
  3. Compute grows combinatorially with fleet size; coordination is the bottleneck.
Centralized control is elegant in theory, but the entire operation lives or dies with one process.

When Things Break

Three failure modes, one shared consequence.

Mode 01

Central Server Crash

A single OS-level failure halts the planner. Every robot freezes mid-path. There is no fallback.

Mode 02

Network Partition

TCP/wireless dropout strands robots without new paths. They either stop or run a stale plan into a wall.

Mode 03

Robot Sensor Fault

A single bot reporting unknown state triggers full-fleet replanning — combinatorial cost.

02/World Models
04 · 23
Definition

A world model is a learned generative simulator.

World-Model Architecture — after Ha & Schmidhuber · 2018
p ( zt+1 | zt, at, ht )
zt+1  next latent state at  chosen action ht  hidden history
"Agents can use the world model to plan a sequence of actions in their dreams." — Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018
  • Trained from observation data alone — no physics engine, no hand-crafted rules.
  • Can be rolled out forward to imagine futures with no simulator in the loop.
  • Train the policy in dreams instead of reality — cheap, parallel, safe.
World model = imagination engine + reward predictor. Together: a self-contained training environment.
02/World Models · Architecture
05 · 23
Three Cognitive Ingredients

Vision · Memory · Controller

V — Perception

Compress pixels to latent

# raw pixels → 64-dim z VAE: (96, 96, 6) → z ∈ ℝ⁶⁴

Variational autoencoder. Stacked conv layers. Reconstruction + KL loss.

M — Imagination

Predict next latent + reward

# dream the next step MDN-RNN: (z, a, h) → ẑ', r̂

LSTM with 5-component Gaussian mixture head. Reward head co-trained.

C — Decision

A tiny linear policy

# 320 → 11 = 3,531 params Linear: [z ⊕ h] → a

One matrix + bias. Trained in dreams via CMA-ES.

V and M are trained offline from data; C is trained inside the world model itself — no real-env steps required.

Component V — Visual Encoder

Compress pixels to a 64-dim latent z.

Input (96, 96, 6) curr + prev frame
▼
Conv 32 · stride 2(47, 47, 32)
Conv 64 · stride 2(22, 22, 64)
Conv 128 · stride 2(10, 10, 128)
Conv 256 · stride 2(4, 4, 256)
▼
FC: μ(64), logσ(64) z latent
▼
Decoder (96, 96, 3) recon current
VAE · Reconstruction sample · epoch 99
  • 6-channel input (frame stacking) → motion cue from a single forward pass.
  • Decoder reconstructs current frame only → forces z to encode current state.
  • Loss: MSE + β·KL (β = 1.0). Training: ~2 h on a consumer GPU.
96² · 6 → 64 ~1 300× compression
03/Methodology · M (Memory)
09 · 23
Component M — Dynamics Model

Imagine the next latent & the next reward.

Input (z, a) ∈ ℝ⁷⁵ 64 + 11
▼
LSTM hidden = 256
▼
gmm_linear → 646
▼
μk, σk, πk, r̂ 5 × 64 + 5 + 1
5-component GMM temperature τ reward head r̂
p (zt+1 | zt, at, ht) = ∑k=15 πk · 𝒩(μk, σk2)
  • 5 Gaussians = "five possible futures, weighted." Multimodal dynamics (e.g., bot at junction).
  • LSTM hidden state ht accumulates history → partial observability.
  • Reward head r̂ — fitness signal CMA-ES uses in dreams.
Live inference · 5 mode-pinned 7-step rollouts (bot 15, mid-warehouse seed)
One seed → five imagined trajectories, each pinned to a different Gaussian mode and weighted by πk. After 7 dream steps, the modes diverge into visibly different futures — the model has genuinely multimodal dynamics.

CMA-ES Training
  • Population: 64 candidate weight vectors per generation.
  • Each evaluated by a dream rollout — no simulator.
  • 50-step rollout · cumulative r̂ = fitness.

Fast policy · live inference

Override-aware action: override = 0 defers to A*; override = 1 picks an explicit direction. The controller intervenes only when beneficial — a hybrid by design.

Reason 01

Physics-grade fidelity

Real motion model — acceleration, deceleration, turning radius. Continuous spatial state, not a grid abstraction. Comparable to real Kiva-style dynamics.

Reason 02

On-board camera support

GymServer streams 96×96×3 RGB from each robot's perspective — identical to what a real robot would see. Critical for our methodology: we train on pixels, not coordinates.

Reason 03

Standard MAPF baselines

Built-in: CBS · BCP · FAR · WHCA* · OD_ID · PAS — for honest benchmarking against the strongest centralized planners.

Reason 04

Open-source & reproducible

C# implementation. Standard benchmark instances. Other groups can replicate or contest our numbers.

Fills the gap between toy gym envs and real warehouses — high fidelity AND visual. Coordinate-only training does not transfer; pixel-trained world models do.

B2/Backup · Dynamics Quality
backup
Anticipated Q · "How do you know dynamics is good?"

Per-dimension Gaussian log-likelihood.

Validation protocol
  • Hold out 20% of episodes.
  • Compute log p(zt+1 | zt, at, ht) under each Gaussian.
  • Average per latent dim — see which dims model well.
Findings
  • Most dims log-likelihood > −2.0.
  • Two dims with bimodal distribution show the largest deviation — these are the multimodal ones (junctions, decisions).
  • Reward head MAE ≈ 0.04 on the validation split.
Per-dim diagnostics tell us where the model fails — and that 5-Gaussian capacity is well-utilised.
B3/Backup · Frame Stacking
backup
Anticipated Q · "Why 6 input channels?"

Without stacking, velocity is invisible.

Setup A · No stacking

Input: (96, 96, 3)

  • Bot stationary looks identical to bot moving.
  • VAE cannot encode motion in z.
  • Dynamics model collapses on motion-dependent transitions.
  • SSIM @ t=10 drops to ~0.42.
Setup B · 2-frame stack (ours)

Input: (96, 96, 6)

  • Velocity inferable from a single forward pass.
  • z encodes both position and motion.
  • Dynamics model handles direction transitions cleanly.
  • SSIM @ t=10 stays at ~0.61.
Frame stacking is the single biggest architectural change vs the original H&S — and it is required.

Why faster overall

Dream rollouts parallelize trivially — no shared simulator state. We saturate CPU cores rather than wait on a single env.

Why scaling is fine

More episodes = more V/M data, but C training cost is independent. The expensive piece is the data collection — and that runs on existing simulator infra.

B6/Backup · CMA-ES Choice
backup
Anticipated Q · "Why not REINFORCE / PPO?"

Action argmax is discontinuous.

Method Action handling Sparse reward Compute
REINFORCEContinuous OKPoorHigh variance
PPO Continuous OKAdequateOnline sim required
CMA-ES Discrete argmax OK Robust Offline-only
  • Our action space contains discrete argmax heads (direction one-hot, accel mode, decel mode) — gradient flow is blocked.
  • CMA-ES sidesteps this by black-box optimisation over the controller's parameters directly.
  • Sparse rewards stay tractable because CMA-ES only needs a cumulative-reward signal per rollout, not per-step credit assignment.
  • 3,531 parameters is a tiny enough search space that ES converges in ~290 generations.
CMA-ES is the right tool for this combination of constraints — discrete actions, sparse reward, offline-only.
